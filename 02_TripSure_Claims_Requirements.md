# TripSure Travel Insurance

## Trip Delay Claim Intake Requirements

**Document owner:** Claims Operations  
**Applies to:** Trip Delay claims submitted under an active TripSure travel protection plan  
**Knowledge-base status:** Approved for guided intake and document-checklist responses  

This document defines the information and evidence used to open and assess a Trip Delay claim. Collect only information necessary for the claim and handle it according to TripSure privacy and security requirements.

---

## TS-CLM-2.1 — Required Claim and Traveler Information

To open a Trip Delay claim, collect:

- TripSure policy or certificate number;
- insured traveler's full name and preferred contact information;
- booking or itinerary number;
- scheduled and actual departure dates and times;
- carrier name, route, and affected flight, train, cruise, or bus number;
- delay location;
- stated cause of the delay;
- total delay duration;
- a concise description of what occurred; and
- whether compensation or reimbursement was requested from or provided by another party.

Before recording sensitive information, confirm the traveler is authorized to discuss the policy. Do not request passwords, full payment-card numbers, bank login credentials, or government account credentials.

If the policy number, insured identity, delay duration, or cause is missing, the assistant may continue collecting available details but must label the intake **incomplete**. The assistant must not infer missing facts from unrelated messages or documents.

---

## TS-CLM-2.2 — Supporting Documentation

Request the following documents when applicable:

- original itinerary and booking confirmation showing scheduled travel;
- carrier notice or written confirmation showing the delay duration and reason;
- boarding passes or other proof of travel;
- itemized receipts for meals, lodging, local transportation, and other claimed expenses;
- proof of payment for each claimed expense;
- documentation of refunds, vouchers, credits, or payments from the carrier or another party;
- police or official incident report for a traffic accident; and
- police, embassy, consulate, or other official report for lost or stolen travel documents.

A credit-card statement alone may show payment but does not replace an itemized receipt when the merchant and purchased items cannot be identified. Screenshots are acceptable for intake when legible, but Claims Operations may request original or higher-quality records.

Missing documentation does not automatically mean the claim is denied. Mark each missing item, explain why it is relevant, and submit the case for human review when the traveler cannot obtain it.

---

## TS-CLM-2.3 — Intake Status, Validation, and Human Review

Use one of these intake statuses:

- **Ready for review:** required claim information is present and applicable supporting documents have been provided.
- **Incomplete — customer action needed:** one or more required facts or documents are missing; identify each missing item.
- **Human review required:** coverage is ambiguous, evidence conflicts, an exception or exclusion may apply, the customer cannot provide standard evidence, fraud indicators are present, or the customer requests a coverage decision.

Before submission, summarize the reported facts and ask the customer to confirm their accuracy. Do not alter customer-provided dates, amounts, or explanations to make them fit policy criteria. Preserve uncertainty using phrases such as “customer reported,” “document not yet provided,” or “cause not confirmed.”

The AI assistant may create or update an intake record and provide a checklist. Only an authorized TripSure claims specialist may determine coverage, request an investigation, approve payment, deny a claim, or communicate a final decision.

**Approved customer wording:** “Your claim intake is ready to be reviewed, but this is not a coverage or payment decision. A TripSure claims specialist will validate the policy, circumstances, and supporting documents.”
